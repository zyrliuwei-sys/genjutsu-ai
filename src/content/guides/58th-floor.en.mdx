>
  What changes in a 58th Floor AI video?
</h2>

The AI version recasts the two roles. A typical two-photo workflow assigns one person to each side of the microphone, then attempts to carry those identities through the performance. That is different from simply animating a selfie: the edit also has to keep track of who is speaking, who is listening and where each person stands.

Template providers already describe this workflow. For example, [Starrd’s 58th Floor guide](https://www.getstarrd.app/blog/how-to-make-58th-floor-ai-video) advertises two-photo versions and a short handoff between the performers. Those are that provider’s features; clip length, sound and identity control vary between tools.

When comparing a template or making your own reference, check these three things:

- **Two separate roles.** A close-up should still show the same person who occupied that role in the wide shot. Do not judge identity from the first frame alone.
- **A believable exchange.** Look for a clear change of performer, with the other person reacting. If both mouths move throughout, the conversation becomes harder to read.
- **A stable scene.** The microphone, backdrop and framing should remain coherent as the people move. Small hand gestures are worth checking frame by frame.

These are review criteria, not promises of perfect likeness or lip-sync. A convincing thumbnail can still hide a weak transition later in the clip.

  How to plan your own 58th Floor edit
</h2>

### 1. Decide whether you need one replacement or two

For a friend-pair remake, look for two explicitly assigned photo slots. Prepare one sharp portrait per person, with similar lighting and an unobstructed face. Label the roles before starting so a close-up cannot quietly switch identities. A group selfie gives the tool less explicit information about which face belongs to which role.

For a single-person experiment, choose a short reference where one performer is clearly the lead. Start with a steady shot before attempting cuts between two faces. You can also film your own duet against a plain teal backdrop to control the framing and timing from the start.

### 2. Choose the moment before you generate

Watch the reference all the way through. Pick a moment with a readable gesture or reaction and leave enough time for it to land. If your editor accepts only a short clip, trim the exact section you want before uploading; otherwise you may lose the exchange that made you choose the scene.

Keep both faces and the hanging mic inside the crop. A vertical crop can remove a performer from a wide duet, so check the full sequence rather than assuming every source fits 9:16.

## Photo and framing mistakes to avoid

- **A face hidden behind glasses or a hand:** choose a clearer reference photo, especially if the result needs a close-up.
- **A headshot used to judge a full-body result:** inspect the clothes, proportions and legs as well as the face. A portrait does not specify an entire outfit.
- **Changing the crop mid-sequence:** keep the intended aspect ratio consistent and make sure both roles remain visible.
- **Expecting a text prompt to reproduce the song:** scene description, identity, motion and audio are different inputs. Check which ones the selected tool actually accepts.
