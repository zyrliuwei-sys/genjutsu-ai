Genjutsu Video AI is useful when you begin with a clear publishing brief. A short for a phone, an ad variation and a B-roll insert need different compositions, even when they share the same subject. Choose the job first, then choose whether you need new footage or a reference-based edit.

## Three ways to plan a clip

### Vertical shorts

A 9:16 clip works best with a readable subject and one action that begins quickly. Leave room for captions and interface overlays. Plan a hook, such as a reveal or a direction change, rather than asking a short generation to tell an entire story.

### Ads and product variations

An ad brief should identify what viewers must notice: the product, the person's expression or the environment. Keep offers, logos and legal copy in your editing software so you can verify them. AI generation is not a guarantee of accurate product geometry or readable lettering.

### B-roll

B-roll supports another shot rather than carrying the whole story. Describe a simple environmental action, a close detail or a camera move with room to cut in and out. Match lighting and aspect ratio to the main edit.

## Ten templates to explore

Template labels do not guarantee an exact effect, soundtrack or shot-for-shot match.

### 1. Genjutsu STORM

Use the central-character and surrounding-crowd reference to study a strong focal point. It is a starting point for a dramatic reveal or character-recasting brief. Preserve one clearly identifiable subject in the character photo.

### 2. Smalltown Boy

The movement-led reference is useful for a travel or street-action hook. Study how forward motion keeps the clip readable, then decide whether your brief needs the existing filmed movement or a completely new scene.

### 3. Burning Bridges

Use the interaction-focused example to plan a reaction or action exchange. Multiple people make identity changes harder to evaluate, so specify which character is the intended replacement rather than assuming every face will change.

### 4. Thriller Dance

This energetic reference is a starting point for performance-driven shorts. Watch the timing and camera rhythm before choosing the source segment. The template name alone does not add a dance or a soundtrack to another uploaded clip.

### 5. Toc Toc

The action-oriented example helps plan a fast-paced cutaway or a high-energy opening. Keep body positions and motion legible; complicated contact and fast occlusions can expose generation artifacts.

### 6. Knight Cat

Use this movement reference when you want momentum through an environment. Compare the direction of travel, camera angle and subject scale with your intended output rather than treating the label as a promise of a cat transformation.

### 7. Car Dance Duo

This label is a starting point for evaluating a two-subject performance brief. If your source contains two people, decide which one should change. One character photo is not a guarantee of independent control over both performers.

### 8. Hotel Lobby

Use the example to plan an arrival, a greeting or an indoor establishing moment. Interior shots benefit from a clear subject separated from the background and lighting that makes the face easy to recognize.

### 9. Vikas Edit

Use this reference to think about a creator-led portrait or transition. Keep the main action simple and check identity consistency throughout the shot, not just in its first frame.

### 10. Alors On Danse

Use this label to explore movement and pacing for a social clip. Choose a segment with one readable action. Add licensed music and final cuts separately rather than expecting the generation to reproduce an existing song.
