Genjutsu Video AI is useful when you begin with a clear publishing brief. A short for a phone, an ad variation and a B-roll insert need different compositions, even when they share the same subject. Choose the job first, then choose whether you need new footage or a reference-based edit.

## Three ways to plan a clip

### Vertical shorts

A 9:16 clip works best with a readable subject and one action that begins quickly. Leave room for captions and interface overlays. Plan a hook, such as a reveal or a direction change, rather than asking a short generation to tell an entire story.

### Ads and product variations

An ad brief should identify what viewers must notice: the product, the person's expression or the environment. Keep offers, logos and legal copy in your editing software so you can verify them. AI generation is not a guarantee of accurate product geometry or readable lettering.

### B-roll

B-roll supports another shot rather than carrying the whole story. Describe a simple environmental action, a close detail or a camera move with room to cut in and out. Match lighting and aspect ratio to the main edit.

### 1. Street Runway (10 s, 16:9)

A model walks toward the camera down a city street and tosses a striped shirt, filmed as a handheld follow. A good fit for fashion and outfit ads where the walk carries the clip.

### 2. Moonlit Walk (5 s, 16:9)

A figure walks away from the camera across mirror-still water under a giant moon. The subject is seen from behind, so it suits atmospheric hooks more than face-led shots.

### 3. Holiday Homecoming (10 s, 16:9)

A man unlocks the door and is greeted by a dog, a child and a decorated Christmas tree. Use it for seasonal campaigns and story-style posts.

### 4. Cosmic Dive (10 s, 16:9)

A young man puts on a glowing visor and jumps into deep space toward a planet. The lead is only briefly in close-up, so choose a photo with a clear, front-facing face.

### 5. Mural Style Shift (4 s, 16:9)

A dancer performs in front of a painted mural while the look switches between art styles on the beat. Short and punchy, it works as an opening or a transition.

### 6. Salt Flat Astronaut (5 s, 9:16)

A woman in a white spacesuit stands on salt flats under a giant planet, helmet under her arm. A vertical portrait shot for fashion or sci-fi themed posts.

### 7. Origami Lookbook (6 s, 9:16)

A group of models in color-block outfits sits beside a giant origami crane; only the model at the front left is recast. Useful for testing a lookbook concept with a group in the frame.

### 8. Sketch Glow-Up (9 s, 9:16)

A girl sketches at a table, flips the page toward the camera and climbs out of it in a magazine-cover look. Your photo is used for both looks, which makes it a natural transformation short.

### 9. Red Suit Groove (10 s, 9:16)

A dancer in a red suit performs in a moody blue hall surrounded by other dancers. Only the lead changes, so the surrounding crowd and choreography stay intact.

### 10. Rail Ollie (10 s, 9:16)

A skater pushes off, ollies over a metal rail and lands in golden-hour light, filmed low and in slow motion. Fast action can expose artifacts, so check hands and feet in the result.
